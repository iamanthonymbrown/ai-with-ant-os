# Template — The Teardown

> **Use for:** A new AI tool or release dropped and you're evaluating where it fits — and where it doesn't.
> **Target length:** 800-1,200 words.
> **Voice:** Field-log. Decisive. Translate the tool, place it inside your OS lens, give the reader a decision.

---

## The structure

What it is (plain English) → Where it fits in your OS → How to actually use it → The call (reach for it / skip it) → What to watch next → CTA.

The "what to watch next" section is what separates a teardown from a review. Reviews tell you about the tool. Teardowns tell you what the tool's trajectory means for the larger system you're building.

---

## The template (copy this block)

```
▶ FIELD NOTES · ISSUE №[00X] · TEARDOWN

# [FILL: Title — the tool or release + your angle. "What [Tool] means for [Audience]" not "[Tool] review."]

[FILL: Subtitle / dek — the verdict or the question this issue answers. One line.]

---

[IMAGE or VIDEO: the tool in action, a screenshot of the output, or a side-by-side with the alternative.]
> GUIDE — Caption + alt text.

## What it is, in plain English
[FILL: Translate it. No jargon, or jargon explained once. 2-3 sentences. Stop when a smart non-technical reader could explain it back to you.]

## Where it fits in your OS
[FILL: The brand lens. Map it to a real phase or workflow inside an AI operating system. Not "good for productivity" — specifically what step it slots into.]

## How to actually use it
[FILL: Concrete. A short workflow or 2-3 steps. Show it deployed, don't just describe it. Include the actual prompt, settings, or configuration.]

[IMAGE/SCREENSHOT optional — caption + alt text]

## The call: reach for it or skip it
[FILL: The decision framework. This is the artifact. Be specific.]

- **Reach for it when:** [FILL]
- **Skip it when:** [FILL]
- **Verdict:** [FILL — one line]

## What I'd watch next
[FILL: One short paragraph on what this tool's evolution unlocks (or threatens). Signals you're tracking the space, not just reviewing in isolation.]

---

[CTA: subscribe, upgrade to paid tier, or whatever fits your funnel]

— [Author]
```

---

## What makes this template work

1. **"Plain English" forces clarity.** Most tool content gets lost in feature lists. Translating to plain language reveals whether the tool actually does something new or whether it's a rebrand.
2. **"Where it fits in your OS" is the lens.** This is the brand differentiator. Generic reviews evaluate tools against generic use cases. The OS lens evaluates them against a specific architecture — which makes the analysis sharper and more useful.
3. **"How to actually use it" includes the literal prompt.** Show it deployed. The reader can copy and paste. This is the "demonstrate, don't explain" rule at the section level.
4. **The call is a decision framework, not a verdict.** "Reach for it when / Skip it when" works for readers across different contexts. A single verdict only helps readers whose context matches yours.
5. **"What I'd watch next" earns repeat readership.** Readers come back to creators who track trajectories, not just snapshots.

## Voice notes

- Decisive. Teardowns are useless if hedged.
- Resist the urge to be evenhanded for its own sake. If the tool is bad, say so. If it's good, say so. If it's good-for-X-but-not-Y, draw the line.
- Avoid press-release vocabulary: "revolutionary," "groundbreaking," "game-changing," "next-level"
- Avoid affiliate-review tells: "I've been testing X for the past few weeks and…" — get to the verdict

## Anti-patterns

- **The fence-sit teardown.** "It depends on your needs." Useless. Pick a side per use case.
- **The feature recap.** Listing what the tool does isn't a teardown — it's a press release rewritten. The teardown is your evaluation.
- **The hype amplification.** If you only post about tools when they're great, you're indistinguishable from the marketing team. Sometimes the call is "skip it."
