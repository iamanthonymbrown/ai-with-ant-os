# Template — The Long Lens

> **Use for:** Industry, economic, or structural takes. Where the AI stack is going, what a market shift means, the second-order effects nobody else is naming.
> **Target length:** 1,200-2,000 words.
> **Voice:** Field-log, but more considered. Analytical without being academic. Still operator-grounded — you're the one looking up from the work, not a pundit broadcasting from above.

---

## Why this template exists

Most operator content stays at the tactical layer — Build Logs, Teardowns, Field Notes. That's where the immediate value lives, but it's also why most operator brands stay locked in their niche. The Long Lens is what builds *enterprise-level authority* over time — the kind of analysis that earns invitations to advise on strategy, not just execute on tactics.

For founders, AEs, PMs, and operators with consulting ambitions: this is the template that signals you can sit across the table from a CIO or VP and have a real strategic conversation. It's the template that compounds across years into a body of work that gets cited.

Don't reach for this every week. Run it when something genuinely structural happens.

---

## The structure

Setup → What most people are missing → The mechanism → Who wins, who loses, what to watch → What this means for the operator → CTA.

The "what this means for the operator" close is the bridge that keeps Long Lens posts from feeling like detached commentary. The operator should leave with one practical decision, even if the decision is "ignore this for six months."

## What makes this template work

1. **Grounded openings.** Long Lens posts that open with a thing-that-happened (an announcement, a deal, a paper, a release, a regulatory shift) earn the reader's time. Long Lens posts that open with abstract claims ("I've been thinking about the future of AI…") lose it.
2. **"What most people are missing" is the operator differentiator.** Pundits write from above. You write from inside the work. Naming what only an operator can see is the trade.
3. **"The mechanism" forces rigor.** You can't write this section by recycling other people's takes. You have to actually think through how the thing works.
4. **Naming winners, losers, and watch signals is what earns enterprise credibility.** Vague predictions ("X will change everything") signal a content creator. Specific predictions tied to verifiable signals signal an analyst.
5. **The "what this means for the operator" close is the trust contract.** Long Lens posts can drift into pure commentary if you let them. Forcing yourself to give the reader one practical decision keeps the post in the brand.

## When to reach for this template

- A major AI release that nobody is interpreting through a systems lens
- An economic or geopolitical shift with second-order effects on the AI stack (data center locations, energy economics, regulation)
- A funding round or acquisition that signals a category move
- A paper or technical breakthrough whose implications haven't been mapped to operators yet
- A structural pattern you've noticed across multiple companies or markets

## When NOT to reach for this template

- You don't actually have a non-obvious take. (Don't fill the space with synthesis of other people's takes.)
- The story is fast-moving and your post will be stale in 48 hours. (That's a [Field Note](field-note.md) or skip.)
- You're trying to sound smart rather than be useful. (Long Lens posts are easy to write badly — the cost of mediocrity is high because the form invites scrutiny.)

## Voice notes

- More considered than Build Logs, but still field-log voice. No academic posturing. No consultant-speak.
- Sentences can run longer here than in other templates, but only when the substance warrants it.
- Use specific company names, role titles, dollar amounts where you can. Specificity is the signal of operator depth.
- Avoid: "ecosystem," "paradigm shift," "step-change," "transformative," "stakeholders" used vaguely
- Replace: "thinks about" → "tracks," "concerns" → "watches," "impacts" → "changes" or "raises" or "kills"
