# Template — The Field Note

> **Use for:** Any lane. One sharp idea developed cleanly. Cadence-keeper for weeks without a big build or a major release.
> **Target length:** 400-700 words.
> **Voice:** Field-log. Tight. One idea, one artifact, no padding.

---

## The structure

Takeaway up front → The setup → The idea → Run it → CTA.

The takeaway-up-front is non-negotiable. If a reader gets two sentences in and leaves, they should have the whole point.

## What makes this template work

1. **"The takeaway, up front" is the bargain.** You tell the reader the whole point in the first beat. In exchange, they read the rest because they trust you not to waste their time. Most short-form posts get this backwards — they bury the lede to hold attention. Operators don't have time for that.
2. **"The setup" is 3-4 sentences, max.** Resist the urge to over-establish. The reader doesn't need your whole week's context. They need just enough to land the idea.
3. **"The idea" is *one* idea.** This is a field note, not an essay. If you have two ideas, write two field notes.
4. **"Run it" is the artifact.** Bullets, one action each. The reader leaves holding something they can do this week.

## When to reach for this template

- You had an insight in the shower and want to ship something this week
- A reader asked a question that deserves a fuller answer than a DM
- A small workflow improvement worth sharing without ceremony
- A pattern you've noticed across multiple recent posts and want to name
- Cadence pressure — you publish weekly, it's Thursday, and the bigger Build Log isn't ready

## When NOT to reach for this template

- You have a substantive build to show → use [Build Log](build-log.md)
- A tool needs evaluating → use [Teardown](teardown.md)
- The idea has industry-level scope → use [The Long Lens](long-lens.md)
- The "idea" is actually three ideas → split it into three field notes

## Voice notes

- The shorter the post, the tighter every word has to be
- One bolded line (the takeaway up front). That's it. No other bolds.
- Avoid throat-clearing openers: "I've been thinking lately about…" — get straight to it
- Avoid the "let me share something I've learned" frame — show the something, don't preview it
